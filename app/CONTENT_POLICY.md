# PWG Content Policy

## Purpose

PrayWithGod.ai creates **original devotional prayers** that are faithful in spirit to a selected tradition without pretending to be scripture, official liturgy, or an authorized sacred translation.

## Core Principle

Outputs must be:
- reverent
- tradition-aware
- original
- devotional

Outputs must **not** be:
- scripture rewrites
- liturgical imitations
- fake canonical prayers
- “new sacred texts”
- authoritative translations
- close paraphrases of known sacred passages

## Allowed

The system may generate:
- original prayers in a tradition-consistent tone
- devotional meditations inspired by a known prayer
- original prayers shaped by a prayer type
- pastoral, contemplative, penitential, thankful, intercessory, or peace-seeking prayers
- gentle personalization using the user’s situation or feelings

## Not Allowed

The system must not:
- quote or closely paraphrase scripture, sutras, mantras, psalms, formal liturgy, or canonized prayers at length
- imitate the signature wording, sequence, or cadence of a famous sacred prayer too closely
- present text as an official translation, revealed text, canonical prayer, or authorized liturgical form
- claim historical or ecclesial authority
- write in a way that suggests the text is itself sacred scripture
- produce “fake chapter/verse” style content
- mimic elevated sacred-text diction so closely that the output reads like a disguised reproduction

## High-Risk Cases

If the requested item is scripture-adjacent, liturgical, or canonically famous, the system should:
- create an **original devotional meditation inspired by its themes**
- avoid hallmark lines and recognizable sequencing
- avoid famous openings and closings
- avoid near-paraphrase

Examples of high-risk material include:
- The Lord’s Prayer / Our Father
- Magnificat / Prayer of Mary
- scriptural prayers of biblical figures when requested as if to be “rewritten”
- Qur’anic-style or sutra-style reproductions
- formal Jewish liturgy rendered as if official
- mantras or chant-like reproductions presented as authentic sacred formulas

## Tradition Guidance

### Christian
May sound prayerful, biblical in spirit, devotional, pastoral, reverent.
Must not become a rewritten psalm, gospel prayer, creed, or official liturgical text.

### Jewish
May sound reverent, communal, covenantal, reflective, praise-filled, penitential, or peaceful.
Must not become formal liturgy, scripture, or an authoritative translation.

### Muslim
May be reverent, humble, God-centered, morally serious, and supplicatory.
Must not resemble Qur’an, pretend to be Qur’anic Arabic in English, or read like revealed scripture.

### Hindu
May be devotional, peaceful, grateful, dharmic, offering-oriented, or contemplative.
Must not pose as mantra, shloka, scripture, or an authoritative sacred rendering.

### Buddhist
May be contemplative, compassionate, aspiration-based, peaceful, and non-grasping.
Must not imitate sutra, canonical chant, or a formal liturgical recitation.

## Style Rules

Outputs should:
- be plain text
- remain clearly human devotional writing
- stay emotionally specific
- avoid generic “AI spirituality”
- avoid fake antiquity or pseudo-scriptural grandeur
- preserve reverence without claiming authority

## Product Language

Use terms like:
- “tradition-faithful rendering”
- “devotional rendering”
- “inspired by”
- “original prayer”

Avoid terms like:
- “official”
- “authorized”
- “translation”
- “revealed”
- “canonical”
- “exact version”
